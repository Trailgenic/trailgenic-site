Foundation Comparison — Walking vs Rucking vs Running

A familiar flat course with actual dose and feeding state declared. Walking supplies the baseline; Rucking adds load; Running changes cardiovascular demand.

The Foundation Comparison is TrailGenic’s repeated-route benchmark. Most sessions use approximately 3.2 miles. The latest records deliberately retain two deviations: fed Walking S26 and the longer 3.86-mile Ruck S19. Those differences prevent treating the current snapshot as a matched experiment.

Walking establishes the low-cost reference. Rucking adds carried weight. Running changes speed, run–walk structure, and cardiovascular intensity. Hiking is analyzed separately as the environmental world model where terrain, weather, altitude, duration, and judgment interact.

Method, provenance and limitations
  1. Distance screening: 3.15–3.25 miles, the reference band declared in this hub. It is not proof of identical exertion.
  2. Fasted reference: exclude an explicitly fed session from the fasted-control inference.
  3. Protocol integrity: mixed run–walk is separated from the steady-running S10–S14 historical block; ruck load blocks also remain distinct.
  4. Passing a screening check would still not establish a controlled experiment. Session date, sleep, pace, hydration and accumulated training vary.

Interpretation

Ella — Longitudinal Pattern Interpretation

The contribution is a repeatable comparison framework with deviations preserved. S26’s fed state and S19’s longer distance make the newest records different tasks. Adaptation should be tested with comparable repeats and linked recovery, not inferred from the lowest whole-session HR.

Rucking records that progressive load from 10 to 20 lb was completed on this route without moving perceived effort above 1 out of 5 or creating anaerobic training effect. Running produces the distinct cardiovascular signature: higher heart rate, higher zones, higher training effect, and protocol-dependent recovery cost.

These controls do not reproduce a laboratory trial. Dates, sleep, pacing, hydration, and the wider training calendar still vary. Their value is narrower and practical: they give Hiking a stable reference for what the system looked like before terrain and environment were added.

The Decision Behind the Comparison

Context → declared dose → response → timed recovery → comparable return. The original asset is a shared-route comparison framework with distinct protocol windows. It is not a randomized or simultaneous experiment.

TrailGenic decision rule · September 24, 2026: Interpret the full trajectory and comparable route or load blocks before the latest session. Preserve the established movement base; adjust a planned dose when repeated comparable responses, symptoms, or recovery trajectories support the change. A single disrupted night or episode does not establish a capacity ceiling. Change one major protocol variable at a time and retain follow-up. Compare within each declared modality before interpreting the combined pattern; HR/distance is not used as a cross-dose efficiency ranking.

HR-per-mile is average session heart rate divided by distance, not total heartbeats per mile or a validated efficiency score. Ella examines patterns and alternative explanations with Mike’s judgment and accountability.

Methodology

  • Course reference — approximately 3.2 miles on a familiar flat route. Actual distance governs comparisons; Ruck S19 is 3.86 miles.
  • Feeding state declared — Walking S1–25, all rucks, and all runs are recorded fasted. Walking S26 is fed and remains a separate protocol observation.
  • Primary variable declared — baseline movement, carried load, or cardiovascular protocol.
  • Like compared with like — running interval sessions are not ranked against steady running; rucking load blocks remain explicit.
  • Environmental context retained — Hiking analyzes environmental complexity directly. Conditions can still confound Foundation comparisons and should remain in the record where available.
