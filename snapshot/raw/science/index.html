Highest-Signal Findings

What the One-Year Record Supports

Lower Longitudinal Cardiovascular Cost

Average hike heart rate fell from 127.6 bpm in the first 19 hikes to 124.1 bpm in the final 19. The later half occurred in warmer conditions at higher average peak elevation; matched-route data strengthen the economy signal.

Lower Garmin Exercise Load

Mean Garmin exercise load fell from 122.2 to 60.8 while maximum heart rate fell from 156.2 to 149.9 bpm. Garmin load is device-generated; the heart-rate direction is the primary finding.

Drift Remains Descriptive

The corrected hiking record contains 32 negative and 6 positive values, with median whole-route drift of −1.0%. Route structure prevents treating the sign as a validated efficiency or fatigue score.

Breath Acetone Rose After Every Paired Hike

Across 37 paired hikes, all 37 post-hike readings were higher. The median rose from 2.2 to 7.3 ppm; the highest reading was 22 ppm. Breath acetone is a directional metabolic proxy—not blood ketones, fat-oxidation rate, autophagic flux, or clinical status.
