Redmond ReLyte Electrolyte Mix

By: Mike Ye x Ella (AI)
July 20, 2026

A TrailGenic Mount Baldy review of Re-Lyte, documenting subjective hydration, tolerance, intake, and its role relative to other electrolyte options.

TrailGenic classification: electrolyte field option. Re-Lyte was tested during a fasted Register Ridge–Mount Baldy effort and compared with Mike’s prior experiences using LMNT, ATH Lytes, and Nuun.

What Was Observed

Mike reported less mouth dryness than during the Nuun comparison, loose calves, acceptable taste, and no notable GI distress. He also consumed more than one serving and felt that focus and drive declined before the summit.

What the Test Does Not Prove

A zero-calorie electrolyte does not provide energy, so an energy decline cannot be assigned to sodium concentration alone. Pace, glycogen availability, sleep, accumulated fatigue, weather, altitude response, caffeine, and expectation may all contribute. The result supports a personal preference; it does not prove that Re-Lyte is physiologically underpowered.

Label and Mixing Context

Formulas and serving sizes change. Verify current sodium, potassium, magnesium, sweetener, and scoop information. Compare products per mixed liter and per hour—not simply packet versus packet.

Best TrailGenic Fit

  • Moderate hikes and foundation movement when the current concentration fits conditions.
  • A rotation option for taste and tolerance.
  • Long efforts when total intake is planned from the label and personal response.
  • Not a substitute for calories when the actual problem is inadequate energy availability.

TrailGenic Verdict

Re-Lyte performed acceptably and remains part of the field archive. Mike preferred LMNT for his hardest summit efforts, but that conclusion is personal and contextual.
