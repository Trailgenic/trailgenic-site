A compact safety reserve carried on long TrailGenic hikes and consumed when changing conditions make continued fasting the wrong priority.

TrailGenic classification: carried safety fuel. The B.T.R. bar is packed for long, exposed, remote, hot, or high-altitude routes where the plan may need to change. It is not consumed automatically. If eaten, it ends the strict fast and the session is logged accordingly.

Why Carry Calories During a Fasted Protocol

Route extensions, slower travel, navigation delays, heat, cold, altitude symptoms, unexpected fatigue, and a long descent can raise energy needs beyond the original plan. Carrying food is risk management—not a lack of discipline.

Field Experience

The bar has been carried on major peaks and consumed when accumulated stress made continued fasting a poor trade. Mike found the compact format convenient and the flavor useful for morale. Its texture can be sticky during exertion, and it is not as rapidly consumed or digested as a gel or simple carbohydrate.

When It Fits

  • A portable reserve when an outing may run long.
  • A deliberate break from the fast when output or judgment is deteriorating.
  • A bridge to a complete meal when the trailhead or drive is still far away.

When Something Else May Be Better

For an urgent need for readily available carbohydrate, a simpler and faster source may be more practical. Persistent confusion, loss of coordination, vomiting, severe weakness, chest symptoms, or worsening altitude illness requires stopping, descending, and seeking help—not relying on a bar to rescue the route.

TrailGenic Verdict

Carry it for safety. If consumed, the fast is over and safety has correctly outranked protocol purity.
