Report 001: Hiking Heart Rate and Recovery in Changing Field Conditions

Status: Published · Version 1.0.0 · November 3, 2026
Authors: Mike Ye; Ella Entity
Method: Longitudinal Pattern Interpretation
License: CC BY 4.0

Report 001 freezes a 38-hike, 93-session publication snapshot. It connects cardiovascular response with recorded temperature, terrain, wind, surface conditions, pace, altitude, and overnight recovery. The package includes the report, public-safe dataset, data dictionary, verified analysis results, and reproduction script.

In development

Report 002: Environmental Load and Cardiovascular Response Across Real-World Hikes

Plan version: 0.1 · November 3, 2026
Planned observation cutoff: November 30, 2026
Target release: December 18, 2026

Report 002 asks how recorded environmental conditions are associated with average heart rate after accounting for pace, elevation gain, peak elevation, and the changing sequence of hikes. Temperature, wind, snow or ice, and technical terrain will be added to a prespecified workload model one at a time to limit overfitting in a small N-of-1 sample.

The current September 30 source snapshot contains 42 hikes. Core workload, cardiovascular, altitude, temperature, terrain, surface, wind, and sun fields are complete. H42 will remain in the descriptive record but will be excluded from the primary model because the ankle injury materially changed pace and descent behavior. The final cohort will be frozen before analysis.

Cadence and version policy

  • Substantive reports are targeted every six to eight weeks.
  • Public-safe dataset releases are targeted quarterly.
  • Material file changes become a new Zenodo version with a transparent history.
  • Minor website wording changes do not create a new research version.
  • Null, mixed, and contradictory findings remain publishable.

Evidence boundaries

This is a longitudinal observational N-of-1 field program. It can identify within-person patterns, generate hypotheses, and improve the next comparison. It cannot establish causation, population-wide effects, medical safety for other people, reduced disease incidence, biological-age reversal, or longer life.

Temperature values are device-recorded field values, not necessarily calibrated ambient exposure. Wind and surface descriptions are qualitative unless an instrument reading is explicitly recorded. Average heart rate is interpreted alongside pace, terrain, route, altitude, and recovery rather than treated as a standalone fitness score.
