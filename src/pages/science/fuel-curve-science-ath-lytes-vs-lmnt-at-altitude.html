Fuel Curve Science: ATH Lytes vs LMNT at Altitude

Evidence Status

Derived synthesis of firsthand N=1 field evidence. This page interprets repeated Mount Baldy observations recorded in TrailGenic field logs. It is not a randomized product trial and should not be used to infer universal electrolyte physiology.

Question

What can TrailGenic reasonably conclude from Mike’s repeated Mount Baldy experiences using ATH Lytes and LMNT under broadly comparable fasted, high-vertical hiking conditions?

Primary Field Evidence

The key source is the Mount Baldy via Register Ridge ATH Lytes field record, interpreted alongside the companion LMNT effort and the later ATH Lytes vs LMNT nutrition review.

Comparison Setup

  • Route family: Mount Baldy / Register Ridge
  • State: fasted
  • Hydration volume: approximately 2 L on the documented ATH session and comparable planned hydration on the LMNT effort
  • Products: ATH Lytes and LMNT
  • Design: repeated-route N=1 observation, not randomized or blinded

Observed Differences

DimensionATH Lytes effortLMNT comparison
Subjective focusEarlier reported declineReported as steadier
Available mix consumedMore consumed by summitMore remained available
Finish timeAbout 15 minutes slowerAbout 15 minutes faster
Session completionCompleted safelyCompleted safely

What Can Be Concluded

On the observed days, LMNT fit Mike’s high-load summit system better. The field evidence supports a personal product preference for those conditions.

What Cannot Be Concluded

The comparison does not isolate sodium as the cause of the observed difference. It does not prove altered glycogen use, plasma-volume preservation, neural firing, oxygen delivery, fat oxidation, autophagy, or universal performance superiority. Weather, pace, sleep, training load, expectation, caffeine, starting hydration, product concentration, and other factors remain potential confounders.

Decision Rule

TrailGenic now chooses electrolytes by composition, current label, conditions, total fluid plan, food intake, tolerance, and prior repeated response—not by a fixed brand hierarchy. For Mike, LMNT remains a preferred option for some longer and harder summit sessions because of his observed field fit. That preference is not a prescription for others.

How to Run a Better Personal Comparison

  1. Use the same familiar route.
  2. Match starting hydration, sleep, food timing, carried fluid, and product concentration as closely as practical.
  3. Record weather, duration, pace, heart-rate context, thirst, GI comfort, perceived exertion, symptoms, and product consumed.
  4. Repeat before calling one session a pattern.
  5. Separate the direct observation from any physiological hypothesis.
