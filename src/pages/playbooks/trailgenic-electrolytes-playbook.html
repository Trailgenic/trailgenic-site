Electrolytes are tools, not a universal control system.

Choose by composition and job, verify the current label, and match the dose to the person and the session—not to a brand hierarchy.

Choose by Composition and Job, Not by Brand Tier

The earlier product hierarchy labeled high-sodium, zero-sugar mixes as the TrailGenic core and gave them the strongest “longevity alignment.” That ranking is retired. Product fit depends on the person, session, environment, food intake, and medical context.

Four Useful Product Jobs

  • Light electrolyte or flavored water: supports palatability during ordinary activity.
  • Higher-mineral mix: may fit substantial sweat loss when the individual context supports it.
  • Oral rehydration formulation: designed for targeted rehydration and used according to product or clinical guidance.
  • Carbohydrate-electrolyte fuel: supplies energy and fluid support during longer or harder work.

How to Compare Labels

Use the serving size, sodium, potassium, magnesium, carbohydrate, caffeine, sweeteners, and other actives. Compare the actual dose consumed, not front-label language such as clean, performance, metabolic, or recovery.

Sugar and Sodium Are Tools

Sugar is not automatically a defect, and sodium is not automatically control. Carbohydrate can be useful fuel. High sodium may fit a specific loss pattern or plan, but it does not make overdrinking safe and is not appropriate for every person.

Evidence Boundary

An electrolyte product does not prove autophagy preservation, fat adaptation, blood-pressure stability, or complete recovery. Symptoms such as cramps, fatigue, or dizziness do not identify a single deficiency.

Engine and Governor

The Engine asks what the task requires. The Governor asks whether product composition, dose, symptoms, weather, medical context, and route consequence make the plan appropriate. These are conceptual lenses.

Personal World Model

Record product, dose, total fluid, food, conditions, symptoms, and function. Use the observation to update a revisable model instead of promoting one brand from an N=1 response.

Attached Product Lists

Any product catalog or PDF should be treated as a dated reference, not a ranking or prescription. Formulas, availability, and pricing change; verify the current label before use.

Step 1 — Read the Label

Record serving size, sodium, potassium, magnesium, carbohydrate, sweeteners, caffeine, and other active ingredients.

Step 2 — Classify the Job

Decide whether the product is primarily water flavoring, mineral replacement, oral rehydration support, or carbohydrate-electrolyte fuel.

Step 3 — Match the Session

Use duration, intensity, heat, cold, altitude, food intake, access to fluid, and prior response rather than a brand ranking.

Step 4 — Start Conservatively

Avoid stacking multiple servings or products before learning the response. More sodium is not automatically more control.

Step 5 — Separate Hydration From Mechanism Claims

Do not claim a product preserves autophagy, creates metabolic purity, or guarantees recovery.

Step 6 — Record Direct Observations

Track amount, timing, fluid volume, conditions, symptoms, thirst, and function.

Step 7 — Revise or Escalate

Adjust from repeated evidence and seek clinical guidance for medical or medication factors that affect fluid and electrolytes.

Comparison Tools

  • Current product labels
  • Serving-size, sodium, carbohydrate, caffeine, and ingredient comparison
  • Session and environmental plan
  • Food, fluid, symptom, and tolerance log

What is the best electrolyte brand?
There is no universal best. Compare composition, task, tolerance, cost, and medical context.

Are zero-sugar products always better?
No. Zero-sugar products can fit some sessions, while carbohydrate-electrolyte products can support longer or more intense work.

Is LMNT the TrailGenic default?
No brand is the universal default. A high-sodium product is one option within a composition-first framework.

Can I use electrolytes every day?
Some people can, but routine need depends on diet, climate, activity, health, and product composition. Daily use is not automatically beneficial.

Do electrolytes improve recovery?
They can help replace fluid and minerals when those were meaningfully lost, but they do not validate a complete recovery state.

Can an oral rehydration solution be used like a sports drink?
ORS products are designed for specific rehydration purposes and differ from ordinary sports drinks. Follow product and clinical guidance.

Does product choice enter the Personal World Model?
Yes—as an input and observation. It should not be converted into a universal score or causal certainty.
