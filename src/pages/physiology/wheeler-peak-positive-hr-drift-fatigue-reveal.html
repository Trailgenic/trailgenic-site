Research correction — one-year audit, September 1, 2026 Wheeler Peak should be read as the final effort in a compressed high-altitude sequence, not as a uniquely diagnostic event. The direct observations remain: 8.57 miles, 2,996 feet of gain, 307 minutes, 13,154 feet peak elevation, 123 bpm average heart rate, 148 bpm maximum heart rate, zero anaerobic training effect, +1.2% whole-route heart-rate drift, and 7.3 ppm post-hike breath acetone. In the complete 38-hike record, Wheeler is one of six positive-drift hikes, and five positive-drift hikes occurred before it. Whole-route drift describes the direction of recorded heart-rate change; route profile, grade, pace, altitude, temperature, hydration, terrain, descent, and recording structure can all contribute. The surrounding sleep and autonomic observations justify contextual caution. They do not prove accumulated recovery debt, recovery failure, or a distinct fatigue-reveal mechanism. Completion proves completion; it does not establish optimality, full absorption, or readiness to repeat.
